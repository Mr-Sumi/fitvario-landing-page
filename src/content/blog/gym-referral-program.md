Referrals are the cheapest way to get members, because a friend's word does the work your advertising would otherwise have to. They happen more when you ask and when the reward is clear.

## Principles that hold across gyms

1. **Reward both people.** The member who refers and the friend who joins should each get something.
2. **Keep it simple.** If you need a paragraph to explain it, it is too complex.
3. **Pay in what costs you little.** Days, add-ons and merchandise cost less than cash.
4. **Make it visible.** A board at the desk and a line on the joining form remind people.
5. **Pay out promptly.** A reward that arrives late is forgotten.

## Reward ideas

| Idea | Referrer gets | Friend gets | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free days | 7 days added to the plan | 7 free days on joining | Costs you only capacity |
| Plan upgrade | A free personal training session | A trial session | Showcases your trainers |
| Add-on | A month of locker or towel service | Waived joining fee | Cheap for you, noticed by members |
| Merchandise | Gym T-shirt or bottle | Same | Members then advertise you |
| Tiers | Bigger reward at 3 referrals | | Rewards your best advocates |

Choose one structure and run it for a quarter. Changing it every month confuses everyone.

## Make tracking painless

Every referral needs to be recorded or the reward becomes an argument. Two easy ways:

- **A referral code:** give each member a short code and ask the new joiner to say it at the desk. Write the code on the joining form.
- **A name on the form:** a field that says "Who referred you?" works if staff fill it in every time.

If your software supports referral codes, use them; Fitvario, for example, lets members share a referral code and rewards them. Otherwise a sheet with referrer, friend, date and reward is enough.

## Stop abuse early

- **Count only paid joiners.** A referral counts when the friend pays, not when they enquire.
- **No self-referrals.** A member cannot refer another account on the same phone number or address.
- **Cap the reward.** A limit per member per quarter avoids runaway costs.
- **Set a cool-off.** Pay the reward after the friend completes their first month, not on day one.
- **Write the terms down.** One short paragraph, kept at the desk and on the form.

## Time the ask

The best moment is after a good experience: the end of a successful first month, a goal reached, or a compliment to a trainer. A trainer saying "bring a friend this week and you both get a free session" feels natural. A blanket message to everyone feels like spam.

## Check whether it pays for itself

Work out your cost per referred member. If the reward is seven free days and a day costs you about ₹40 of capacity, the cost is a few hundred rupees. If that member then pays ₹1,200 a month for several months, the programme is cheap. Compare it with what you spend on other marketing, and project the effect with the [revenue calculator](/tools/gym-revenue-calculator).

## A worked example

Suppose 20 members make one referral each in a quarter, and 12 of those friends join. If each referrer and each friend gets seven free days, you have given away 168 days. If the 12 new members each stay six months at ₹1,200, that is ₹86,400 of fees. These numbers are illustrative, and the logic is the part to keep: count joiners, count what you gave, compare.

## Common mistakes

- Making the reward so large that it attracts low-quality referrals.
- Rewarding the enquiry rather than the joining.
- Forgetting to tell new members the programme exists.
- Not following up the friend. A referred lead is warm, so use the [follow-up script](/blog/gym-lead-follow-up-script) the same day.

Referrals are one method among several. See the others in [how to get more gym members](/blog/get-more-gym-members-india).

## Wording you can use

Ask in person, then follow with a short message.

> Hi {name}, thank you for your support. If you bring a friend this month, you both get {reward}. Just ask them to say your name or code at the desk.

For the friend, when they enquire:

> Hi {name}, {referrer} told us about you. As their friend you get {reward} when you join. Would you like to visit this week?

## Questions owners ask

### Should the reward be cash?

Cash is easy to understand and also easy to abuse. Free days, add-ons and merchandise cost you less, are harder to game and keep the member inside your gym. Use cash only if you have a way to verify and cap it.

### What if two members claim the same friend?

Decide a rule in advance: the first recorded referral counts. Write the code or name on the joining form on the day, so there is no argument later.

### Do referral programmes work for small gyms?

They work well, because small gyms have close ties between members. The smaller your member base, the more important it is to keep the ask personal and the reward quick.

### How long should a campaign run?

A quarter is a good length. It is long enough to see results and short enough to refresh. Announce the end date, so the programme has a rhythm and members act before it closes.
