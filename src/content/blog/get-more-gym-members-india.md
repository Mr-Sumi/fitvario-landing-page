Most gyms look for more members before they have fixed how they treat the ones who already enquire. Start with the basics below. They cost little and they compound.

## 1. Be easy to find on Google Maps

When someone searches "gym near me", your Google Business Profile is what they see. Make sure it has your correct address and timings, a phone number that someone answers, recent photos of the floor, and a clear list of services. Ask happy members for reviews by sharing the link, and reply to every review, including the poor ones, politely.

## 2. Show the gym on Instagram, honestly

Short clips of a class, a trainer demonstrating a movement or a member milestone do better than stock-style posts. Post what a visitor would actually see. Put your location and timing in the bio and keep a link or a number people can message. Do not buy followers; they do not walk in.

## 3. Make trying you easy

A first-time visitor is nervous about price and about looking out of place. Remove both barriers. Offer a one-day or three-day trial pass for a small fee or for free, with a trainer to show them around. The pass should cost you little, and it lets a person decide with less risk.

## 4. Follow up every enquiry

Most enquiries come in by phone, walk-in or message and then go cold because nobody called back. Write down every enquiry with a phone number and a follow-up date, and make the follow-up call the next day. The [follow-up script](/blog/gym-lead-follow-up-script) gives you wording. A list that shows who is due today, as in the [gym CRM](/features/gym-crm), keeps this from slipping.

## 5. Ask for referrals, and reward them

Your members are your best marketing, but they rarely recommend you unprompted. Ask at the right moment, such as after a good month, and give something small to both sides. The [referral programme guide](/blog/gym-referral-program) covers reward structures and how to avoid abuse.

## 6. Work with your neighbourhood

Look for groups of people who already live or work nearby: apartment societies, offices, schools, coaching centres, doctors and physiotherapists. A short talk, a group discount or a corporate batch brings several members in one visit. Keep it simple: one contact person, one offer, one end date.

## 7. Use WhatsApp for conversation, not broadcast

Many people would rather reply on WhatsApp than take a call. Use it to answer questions quickly, to send timings and the trial-pass details, and to share a photo of the gym. Do not add people to groups without asking, and keep promotional messages occasional.

## Measure what works

Ask every new member how they found you and write it down. After a month you will see which source brings people in and which is only noise. Compare the number of enquiries with the number who joined. If many enquire and few join, the problem is your follow-up or your trial, not your marketing.

| Source | What to track |
| --- | --- |
| Google Maps | Calls and direction requests |
| Instagram | Messages and walk-ins who mention it |
| Referrals | Joiners per referring member |
| Societies and offices | Joiners per visit or talk |

## A simple first month

Week 1: fix the Google profile and write down every enquiry. Week 2: start the trial-pass offer and the next-day follow-up call. Week 3: ask ten happy members for a referral and a review. Week 4: contact two local groups with a clear offer, then review which source gave results.

Growth depends on keeping people too. When you are ready, read how to [reduce member churn](/blog/reduce-gym-member-churn-india), and check how many members you need with the [break-even calculator](/tools/gym-break-even-calculator).

## What not to do

- **Do not discount first.** A price cut brings people who compare on price alone, and they leave for the next cheaper offer. Fix visibility and follow-up before you touch the fee.
- **Do not spend before you can answer enquiries.** Advertising that sends calls to a phone nobody picks up wastes money twice.
- **Do not copy what a big chain posts.** Their audience and budget are different from yours. Show your own floor and your own members.
- **Do not hide the price.** People who ask "how much?" and get "come and see" often do not come. Give a range and invite them to visit for the details.

## A realistic budget view

Start with time, not money. Fixing your Google profile, writing down enquiries and calling back next day cost nothing but discipline. Add small spends once you know what works: a printed pamphlet for a nearby society, a boosted post for a trial-pass offer, a banner that shows your timings. Compare the cost of each source with the number of members it brought, not the number of likes.

## Questions owners ask

### Which method works fastest?

Following up every enquiry and offering a trial pass usually shows results in weeks, because both fix leaks you already have. Maps and reviews build more slowly, and referrals depend on how many happy members you have.

### Do I need a website?

A simple page with your address, timings, plans and a phone number helps, and many people will see it only through your Google profile. It does not need to be elaborate. A clear, current Google Business Profile does most of the work.

### How many new members should I aim for each month?

Work backwards from churn. If you lose 10% of members every month, you need to enrol that many just to stand still, and more to grow. Use the [churn calculator](/tools/gym-churn-calculator) to find your number.
