> Licences, fire and building rules, and tax treatment differ by state and city and change over time. Every legal, tax and cost point below needs to be checked locally with your authority and your CA before you act. This guide is general information, not professional advice.

## 1. Decide who you are building for

A gym for college students in a tier 2 city is a very different business from a premium studio in a metro. Decide the audience first: age, income, what they want and what they already pay. It sets your location, equipment, price and tone. Spend a week visiting gyms nearby as a customer and note what they do well and what they do not.

## 2. Pick a location

Look for footfall of the people you want, easy access, parking or a safe place for two-wheelers, and a lease that gives you time to build. Check the landlord's terms on rent increases and on what you may change inside. Visit at the hours your members would train.

## 3. Plan the space

Area decides capacity, and capacity decides how many members you can serve at peak time. Leave room for cardio, strength, free weights, stretching and a reception. Think about ventilation, flooring, lighting, changing rooms and an emergency exit. Verify the building and fire requirements locally.

## 4. Choose equipment

Start with what your audience will use most. Buy from reliable suppliers, check the warranty and service arrangements, and compare buying with leasing. A smaller floor with well-maintained equipment serves members better than a crowded one with broken machines.

## 5. Budget honestly

Add your one-time costs (equipment, interiors, deposit, licences, branding, software, pre-launch marketing), then months of running cost to carry you until the gym fills, then a buffer for the unexpected. The [set-up cost calculator](/tools/gym-startup-cost-calculator) lays this out. Then run the numbers for your monthly break-even with the [break-even calculator](/tools/gym-break-even-calculator). If you need more members than your area can supply, change the plan before you spend.

## 6. Handle licences and registrations

Typical areas to check are local trade or shop licences, fire and building clearances, business registration, tax registration and any music or signage permissions. The exact list and the order depend on your location. Ask the local authority, and keep copies of everything.

## 7. Hire and train

A small gym needs a manager or owner on the floor, one or two trainers and a front-desk person. Hire for attitude and teach the rest. Write down the opening routine, the cleaning routine and what to do in an emergency.

## 8. Set your prices

Price from your cost per member, and build a ladder of plans. Our [pricing guide](/blog/gym-membership-pricing-india) walks through the steps. Decide in advance about joining fees, freezes and refunds, and put them in writing.

## 9. Get ready to market

Before opening, set up your Google Business Profile, make an Instagram page, print a simple brochure and plan a pre-launch offer. Talk to the societies and offices around you. Then plan how every enquiry will be recorded and followed up. Most opening-month leads are lost to a missed call-back.

## 10. Your first 90 days

| Period | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Days 1 to 30 | Open, collect feedback, record every enquiry, fix small problems |
| Days 31 to 60 | First renewals, referral ask, first reviews |
| Days 61 to 90 | Check break-even, adjust prices and timings, plan the next quarter |

Track members, enquiries, conversions and collections weekly from day one.

## 11. Common mistakes

- Spending the whole budget on equipment and leaving nothing for running costs.
- Opening before the licences are in place.
- Pricing to match a rival without knowing your own cost.
- Not recording enquiries.
- Hiring a trainer and expecting them to also run the desk and the sales.

## Roles in a small gym

| Role | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| Owner or manager | Money, hiring, member experience, and the weekly review |
| Trainer | Floor supervision, programmes, personal training |
| Front desk | Joining, payments, enquiries, follow-up |
| Cleaner or housekeeping | Cleanliness, which members notice first |

In a very small gym one person may hold two roles, but write the duties down so nothing falls between them.

## Questions people ask before opening

### How big a space do I need?

It depends on how many members you want at peak time and what you offer. Start from your target audience and the equipment they will use, then check that the space leaves room to move. A crowded floor is a common reason for early churn.

### Should I buy new or used equipment?

New equipment carries a warranty and service support. Used equipment costs less but can fail sooner and may lack spares. Whatever you choose, ask who services it, how quickly, and at what cost.

### How long until a gym breaks even?

It varies too much to give a single answer. Work it out from your own costs and your own sign-up rate with the [break-even calculator](/tools/gym-break-even-calculator), and test it with pessimistic numbers.

### Do I need software from day one?

You need a reliable way to record members, payments and enquiries from the first day, because lost records are hard to rebuild. A spreadsheet is a start. Move to software when the list becomes hard to keep accurate.

### What should I do in the month before opening?

Complete the licences, test the equipment, train the team, set up your Google profile, hold a small preview for friends and neighbours, and set the opening offer. Record every enquiry from that week onwards.
